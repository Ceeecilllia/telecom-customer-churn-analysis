# Business insights and retention actions

## Executive summary

The company has a 26.54% snapshot churn rate: 1,869 of 7,043 customers are marked as churned. Their source monthly charges sum to approximately $137.1K, representing a practical monthly revenue-exposure proxy. The strongest descriptive divide is contract duration. Month-to-month customers churn at 45.84%, compared with 10.71% for one-year and 2.55% for two-year customers.

## Priority findings

### 1. Contract flexibility is associated with substantially higher churn

Month-to-month customers account for 1,655 of 1,869 churned customers. Rather than applying a blanket discount, the business should test targeted migration offers for customers who show additional risk signals, such as short tenure, high charges, or fiber-optic service.

**Suggested test:** compare a one-year migration credit, a price-lock guarantee, and a control group. Track acceptance, 30/90-day retention, incremental margin, and discount cost.

### 2. The highest-risk service combination is month-to-month plus fiber optic

This combination has a 58.82% observed churn rate. The pattern may reflect price, service expectations, reliability, or other correlated characteristics; the snapshot alone cannot identify the cause.

**Suggested action:** combine targeted customer outreach with service-quality diagnostics and reason-coded follow-up. Separate pricing complaints from reliability and support issues before selecting an intervention.

### 3. Early-tenure, high-charge customers should receive proactive onboarding

The scatter analysis shows a visible group with tenure below 12 months and validated monthly charges of at least $70. Early intervention is operationally useful because the business can address setup friction and expectation gaps before the relationship matures.

**Suggested action:** trigger a 30/60/90-day onboarding sequence, service-health check, and bill-explanation message for this group.

### 4. Competitor-related reasons dominate recorded churn explanations

“Competitor had better devices” and “Competitor made better offer” are the two largest individual recorded reasons, with 313 and 311 customers respectively. Together they account for one-third of churned customers.

**Suggested action:** create a competitor-save playbook with device upgrade paths, targeted offer matching, and escalation rules. Measure save rate and retained contribution margin, not offer acceptance alone.

## Prioritization framework

The project uses an interpretable descriptive score based on four observable signals:

1. Month-to-month contract
2. Tenure below 12 months
3. Validated monthly charge of at least $70
4. Fiber-optic service

Customers with three or four signals are labelled **High** priority, two signals **Medium**, and zero or one signal **Low**. This score is intended for transparent operational triage; it is not a predicted churn probability.

## Measurement plan

Retention initiatives should be evaluated with holdout or randomized-control groups where feasible. Recommended outcome metrics are incremental retention, incremental gross margin, cost per retained customer, offer take-up, and customer complaint rate. This avoids interpreting descriptive correlations as causal evidence.
